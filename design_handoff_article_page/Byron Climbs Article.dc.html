First Squamish trip of the year

This was the first trip to Squamish this season — and if the weather holds, the first of many. I love this place in a way I still can't quite put into words. Something about the way the granite meets the ocean, the smell of cedar coming off the parking lot, the low grey light sitting on the Sound before the sun finds its way over the Chief.

We rolled in late Friday, set up camp in the dark, and woke to that particular Squamish silence — no wind, no birds yet, just the river somewhere below the trees. The plan was loose: warm up on the Apron boulders, then walk out to the water and see if Majestic was dry.

Majestic is the reason I keep coming back. It's a deep water solo — a single, perfect line of granite rising straight out of the Pacific. No rope, no pads, just you and forty feet of cold ocean waiting if you blow it. I'd stared at photos of it for years. Standing underneath it for the first time, the photos felt like a lie. It is so much bigger in person.

I spent the morning just touching holds, working out the lower moves where a fall only means a swim. The climbing is incredible — long pulls between rounded, glacier-polished edges, feet smearing on nothing, the whole wall overhanging just enough to keep you honest.

Forty feet up, the Pacific swallowing the light below me, and nothing left to do but trust the next move.

By afternoon I'd linked it to the last hard move: a committing throw to a sloping rail at the lip, then a mantel onto the top. I fell trying to mantel. Forty feet, a half-second of absolutely nothing, and then the ocean — colder than I expected and louder than I expected, the kind of cold that resets your whole nervous system.

I swam back to the rocks laughing. Didn't get the send — fell at the very top trying to commit to that mantel — but I'm not sure I've ever been happier to fail at something. That last move is going to live rent-free in my head all winter.

Which, of course, just means I have to come back. I always have to come back. That's the deal with this place.
